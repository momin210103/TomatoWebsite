THE RESEARCH

Accuracy matters.
Understanding matters too.

Deep learning can classify leaf symptoms, but a high score alone does not explain why a model made a decision. This study compares five pretrained CNNs, evaluates performance beyond a controlled dataset, and uses Grad-CAM to visualize influential image regions.

ABOUT THE STUDY

Research with purpose.

This thesis explores an accurate, computationally practical, and interpretable approach to tomato leaf disease classification.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Making deep learning more understandable.

Tomato plants are affected by fungal, bacterial, and viral diseases that can reduce crop quality and yield. This research investigates transfer learning for classifying tomato leaf images while adding visual explanations to help people inspect model behavior.

The study compares VGG16, AlexNet, ResNet50, EfficientNet-B0, and DenseNet121 under the reported experimental setup. DenseNet121 is selected as the proposed model based on its overall classification results, then evaluated on an independent PlantDoc dataset and examined with Grad-CAM.

Research objectives

  1. Compare five pretrained CNN architectures across ten tomato leaf categories.
  2. Balance classification performance with model size and inference cost.
  3. Use preprocessing and augmentation to support generalization.
  4. Evaluate cross-dataset generalization from PlantVillage to PlantDoc.
  5. Use Grad-CAM to visualize image regions influencing predictions.
